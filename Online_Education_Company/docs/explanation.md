**Approach.** I started from the reviewers' own notes (feedbacks.pdf), not generic teaching theory: rapport, asking children individually, energy, homework/conclusion, visible face and lighting. The app scores six weighted dimensions from signals computed locally (ffmpeg, OpenCV, numpy): talk time, turns, reply latency and monologues from the speaker-labelled transcript; speech ratio and dead air from audio; face, shared screen, camera-off and frozen video from frames. Every observation carries a timestamp and quote, e.g. "teacher talked on 32:47-50:11 while no child spoke". Recordings stay local and are deleted after processing; the optional LLM narrative is off by default and sees only anonymised text.

**Judgement calls.** "Quality issue" classes are setup or platform faults, not teaching grades, so they are reported in a separate panel; platform faults (dropouts, frozen video) are not held against the teacher. The Malayalam transcript is garbled, so keyword checks switch off when the transcript is not mostly English.

**What worked.** Interaction signals (child turns per minute, reply latency, longest silent stretch, speech ratio) separate Excellent clearly: both score 82-86, everything else 71 or below. Explanations echo the reviewers' remarks (no rapport, dim face, no homework).

**What failed.** Leave-one-out accuracy is only 4/8 exact (6/8 within one class; chance about 2/8). A Poor class (B558) scores 71 because its faults are tone and structure, which keyword matching cannot see. Teacher pitch variation, tried as an "energy" proxy, carried no signal and was dropped. Reading outputs exposed bugs I fixed ("Good evening" counted as praise).

**Limitations.** Only 8 labelled classes, so cut-offs are hand-set and Poor/Average/Good are weakly supported. Tone and the 8x8 background are not measured. Without a transcript there are no speaker labels, so interaction cannot be judged; local Whisper is slow on free hosting and weaker on Malayalam, and I could not test it on real audio here.
