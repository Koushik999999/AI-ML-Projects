YouTube Shorts Performance Prediction¶

Project Objectives¶

  • Analyze engagement patterns across YouTube Shorts.
  • Calculate Engagement Rate and create performance categories (Low, Medium, High).
  • Perform exploratory data analysis to identify key trends and relationships.
  • Engineer new features from video metadata and title characteristics.
  • Evaluate the impact of duration, upload timing, and content category on engagement.
  • Build and compare multiple machine learning classification models.
  • Select the best-performing model using Cross-Validation, F1-Macro, Accuracy, and ROC-AUC.
  • Generate actionable recommendations to help creators maximize the viral potential of their Shorts.

q1 = df['engagement_rate'].quantile(0.33)
q2 = df['engagement_rate'].quantile(0.66)

def classify_engagement(x):
    if x <= q1:
        return 'Low'
    elif x <= q2:
        return 'Medium'
    else:
        return 'High'

df['performance_engagement_tertile'] = \
    df['engagement_rate'].apply(classify_engagement)

Machine Learning Model Development¶

After completing feature engineering and exploratory data analysis, multiple classification models are trained to predict whether a YouTube Short belongs to the Low, Medium, or High engagement category.

The models are evaluated using:

  • Accuracy
  • F1-Macro Score
  • Cross Validation
  • ROC-AUC
  • Confusion Matrix

The best-performing model will be selected based on its ability to generalize and correctly classify all engagement categories.

In [20]:
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder, StandardScaler
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    X,
    y,
    test_size=0.20,
    random_state=42,
    stratify=y
)

categorical_features = X.select_dtypes(include=['object']).columns.tolist()
numerical_features = X.select_dtypes(exclude=['object']).columns.tolist()

preprocessor = ColumnTransformer(
    transformers=[
        (
            'num',
            Pipeline([
                ('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='median')),
                ('scaler', StandardScaler())
            ]),
            numerical_features
        ),
        (
            'cat',
            Pipeline([
                ('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='most_frequent')),
                ('onehot', OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore'))
            ]),
            categorical_features
        )
    ]
)

Cross Validation Comparison¶

Cross-validation is performed using Stratified K-Folds to obtain a reliable estimate of model performance. F1-Macro is used because it gives equal importance to all three engagement classes.

In [22]:
from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold, cross_val_score

cv = StratifiedKFold(
    n_splits=5,
    shuffle=True,
    random_state=42
)

cv_results = []

for name, model in models.items():

    pipeline = Pipeline([
        ('preprocessor', preprocessor),
        ('model', model)
    ])

    accuracy = cross_val_score(
        pipeline,
        X_train,
        y_train,
        cv=cv,
        scoring='accuracy'
    )

    f1 = cross_val_score(
        pipeline,
        X_train,
        y_train,
        cv=cv,
        scoring='f1_macro'
    )

    cv_results.append([
        name,
        accuracy.mean(),
        accuracy.std(),
        f1.mean(),
        f1.std()
    ])

cv_results_df = pd.DataFrame(
    cv_results,
    columns=[
        'Model',
        'CV Accuracy Mean',
        'CV Accuracy Std',
        'CV F1 Macro Mean',
        'CV F1 Macro Std'
    ]
)

cv_results_df.sort_values(
    by='CV F1 Macro Mean',
    ascending=False
)

Question 1. Distribution of the Target Variable and Class Imbalance Analysis¶

The Engagement Rate was calculated as:

Engagement-Rate = (Likes + Comments + Shares)/(Views)

Videos were categorized into three performance groups (Low, Medium, and High) using tertile-based segmentation. Since tertiles divide the dataset into approximately equal-sized groups, the resulting target distribution is nearly balanced.

Observations:

  • Low Performance: ~33% of videos
  • Medium Performance: ~33% of videos
  • High Performance: ~33% of videos

Business Interpretation: The target variable does not exhibit significant class imbalance. This is beneficial because machine learning models can learn all performance categories equally well without being biased toward a dominant class.

Modeling Impact: A balanced target distribution improves the reliability of evaluation metrics such as Accuracy, F1-Macro, and ROC-AUC and reduces the need for resampling techniques.

Question 2. Impact of Video Duration on Engagement Rate¶

To understand the relationship between video duration and engagement, a scatter plot was created between duration_sec and engagement_rate.

Key Findings¶

  • Video durations ranged from approximately 5 to 60 seconds.
  • Most videos exhibited engagement rates below 2 regardless of duration.
  • A few viral outliers achieved exceptionally high engagement rates, primarily around the 35–45 second range.
  • The correlation matrix shows a very weak relationship between duration_sec and engagement_rate (approximately 0.02), indicating little linear dependence.

Business Interpretation¶

There is no strong evidence that simply making a video longer or shorter guarantees higher engagement. However, several of the highest-performing Shorts appear within the 35–45 second duration range, suggesting that this duration may provide enough time to deliver valuable content while retaining viewer attention.

Model Reliance¶

The correlation analysis indicates that duration contributes only marginally to engagement prediction. The model relies more heavily on engagement-related behavioral features than on video length itself.

Recommendation¶

Creators should focus primarily on content quality and audience relevance rather than optimizing for a specific duration. A target range of 35–45 seconds may provide a slight advantage while maintaining viewer retention.

Question 8. Actionable Recommendations for YouTube Creators¶

Based on exploratory analysis, predictive modeling, and model explainability, the following recommendations can immediately improve the probability of creating a high-performing YouTube Short.

1. Focus on Lifestyle, Travel, and Tech Content¶

These categories consistently generated the highest average engagement rates and therefore offer the greatest opportunity for audience growth.

2. Publish During High-Engagement Time Windows¶

The most effective upload periods were:

  • 3 AM – 5 AM
  • 9 AM – 11 AM
  • Around 10 PM

Scheduling uploads during these windows can improve early engagement and algorithmic visibility.

3. Optimize Content for Shares and Comments¶

The strongest predictive features were engagement intensity metrics such as:

  • Shares per second
  • Comments per second
  • Likes per second

Creators should encourage interaction through questions, challenges, and discussion prompts.

4. Keep Videos Concise and Engaging¶

While duration showed only a weak relationship with engagement, many high-performing Shorts fell within the 35–45 second range. Content should be concise while delivering clear value.

5. Monitor Engagement Velocity¶

Rapid likes, comments, and shares shortly after publication are strong indicators of viral potential. Creators should focus on maximizing early audience interaction through compelling hooks and strong calls-to-action.

Final Conclusion¶

The analysis demonstrates that audience interaction signals are far more important than simple video characteristics. Creators who publish at optimal times, focus on high-performing categories, and actively encourage engagement are significantly more likely to produce viral Shorts.
